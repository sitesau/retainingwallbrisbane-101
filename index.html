Retaining Wall Builders Brisbane Comparison Explained

Retaining walls are priced on the face area of the wall, height times length, not on lineal metres. That is why a 10 metre wall at 600 mm and a 10 metre wall at 1.4 metres are different jobs. As an indicative guide, timber sleeper sits around $300 to $450 per square metre and concrete sleeper walls at about $550 to $700 per square metre, for materials and labour only, excluding GST and excavation. On a sloping block, excavation can be a major reason one price sits well above another. Retaining wall builders Brisbane use the same face area basis quoters use. This means that when you compare quotes, you must ensure every builder is measuring the same face area and accounting for the same excavation depth and drainage complexity.

The five approval triggers

The familiar shorthand, "over a metre needs council approval", is only the first of five conditions. Under the Building Regulation 2021 and the Queensland Development Code, a wall avoids building approval only if it clears every one of the following. Brisbane City Council sets the same triggers out on its retaining wall page.

  • Total height over 1.0 metre
  • Any surcharge load, at any height
  • Within 1.5 metres of a structure
  • Part of a pool barrier
  • Wall plus fence 2.0 metres or more

Approval and licensing are separate questions. Building approval turns on those physical triggers; QBCC licensing turns on the dollar value of the work. Where approval applies, the wall is generally designed to AS 4678 by an RPEQ engineer and certified on Form 15.

Matching material to the structural job

Material is usually the first thing people try to decide and the last thing that should be locked in. Height, surcharge and drainage set the engineering, then material follows from the site, access and budget. Concrete sleeper walls are presented as the workhorse option on Brisbane cut and fill blocks, and as the usual replacement when an older timber wall gives out. Timber sleeper walls still suit low garden terraces, low boundary retaining and stepped beds on Brisbane's inner-west slopes. Core filled block is positioned for taller engineered loads and rendered finishes. Rock, boulder and gabion walls are described as better suited to larger or steeper sites, with free draining behaviour specifically noted for rock and gabion systems. Where quotes diverge early, ask whether each one assumes the same drainage detail, excavation effort and stormwater connection, not just the same wall face.

Why drainage matters

Behind every one of them, subsoil drainage is the part that decides whether the wall is still straight in fifteen years. Concrete sleeper walls, steel-reinforced planks slotted between galvanised posts, do most of the work on Brisbane cut-and-fill blocks. Timber sleeper walls still suit low garden terraces. Core-filled block carries taller engineered loads and takes a render. Sandstone, boulder and gabion walls sit better on larger, steeper sites and drain freely by nature. Agi drain, gravel and geofabric behind every wall, connected to approved stormwater, is the structural detail that stops failures in Brisbane's reactive clay. Without proper drainage, even a well-engineered wall can fail over time due to water pressure behind the structure.

  1. Measure face area. Calculate the wall height multiplied by the total length to determine the face area. This is the standard basis for all retaining wall quotes.
  2. Identify surcharge loads. Check if anything sits on the ground above the wall, such as a driveway, building, pool or second wall. These loads can trigger approval requirements even on low walls.
  3. Confirm drainage requirements. Ask every builder if Agi drain, gravel and geofabric are included. Proper drainage behind the wall is essential to prevent failure in Brisbane's reactive clay.

Common questions

Do I need council approval for a low wall? Only if it meets one of the five triggers, such as being within 1.5m of a structure, carrying a surcharge load, or reaching 2.0m combined with a fence.

Why are quotes so different? Quotes differ based on face area, excavation depth, drainage complexity, and whether engineering or council approval is required.
